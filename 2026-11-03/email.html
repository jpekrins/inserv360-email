Did you really miss a package?

Your phone buzzes. "Your package could not be delivered. Update your address here."

You did order something last week... right? So you tap the link.

The truth is, that's exactly what the scammers are counting on. Holiday shipping season is their favorite time of year. More packages means more people who won't think twice.

Here's how the scam works:

1
The text looks official.
It uses names like USPS, UPS, FedEx, or Amazon. It might even have a real-looking tracking number.
2
The link goes to a fake site.
It's a copy of the real thing. It asks you to "confirm" your address, pay a small redelivery fee, or log in.
3
They steal what you type.
Your card number. Your password. Sometimes it's a work login, and that's how a text on your phone turns into a breach at your company.

Let's fix it. Your 5-second check:

  • Don't tap the link. Ever. Even if it looks real.
  • Go to the source. Open the carrier's app or type their website yourself. If there's a real problem, it'll be there.
  • Watch for pressure. "Act now" or "your package will be returned" is a red flag.
  • Report it, then delete it. Forward scam texts to 7726 (it spells SPAM), then delete.

The best part? Once you know the trick, you'll spot these in seconds.
