Is that ad really from the company?

You need to log in to a vendor's website. Or find a support number. Or grab a Black Friday deal. So you search for it.

The very first thing you see is a link with the company's name and logo. Easy, right? You click.

The truth is, that link might not be the company at all. The FTC just warned that scammers are buying ads that pretend to be well-known businesses and government agencies. In its words: "now scammers are paying too — and people are losing big money."

Here's how the scam works:

1
They buy an ad that looks real.
Same name. Same logo. It shows up in search results, your social media feed, and even on shopping sites.
2
The ad sends you to a copycat site.
It looks just like the real one. But the web address is a little off. The FTC says fake sites copying its own website have ended in .com, .org, .us, or .online, while the real ones always end in .gov.
3
They grab what you type or pay.
Your password. Your card number. Your work login. Or a "support" number that connects you straight to a scammer. That's how one quick click turns into a big problem.

Let's fix it. Your 4-step check:

  • Skip the ads. Scroll past anything marked "Sponsored" or "Ad" when you're looking for a login, a support number, or a place to pay.
  • Go straight to the source. Use a bookmark, or type the web address yourself. For a phone number, check your bill, your contract, or the back of your card.
  • Check the address bar. Before you type a password or card number, look at the web address. Wrong spelling or wrong ending? Close it.
  • Clicked one already? Don't panic. If you typed a work password, call us right away. Then report the scam at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

The best part? A bookmark takes two seconds to make, and it beats a fake ad every single time.

The FTC says it got more than 1 million reports about imposter scams last year, with $3.5 billion in reported losses. Don't let your team be part of next year's number.
