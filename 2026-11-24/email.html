"I'm in trouble. Please don't tell anyone."

Your phone rings. It's your grandson. Or your niece. Or your kid away at college.

They sound scared. They need money right now. Bail. A car repair. And please, don't tell Mom.

Would you stop and think? Or would you just help?

The truth is, that voice might not be them at all. The FTC just warned that scammers can now clone a loved one's voice and even fake a video. The FBI has warned about the same thing.

Here's how the scam works:

1
They do their homework.
Scammers pull details from social media, like where you live and who's in your family. That makes the story feel real.
2
They make you panic.
Someone you love is in trouble. You need to act fast. And you need to keep it a secret. Why? So you send money before you can think it through or talk to anyone.
3
They want hard-to-trace money.
A wire transfer. Crypto. A payment app. Or the numbers off the back of a gift card. Once it's sent, it's very hard to get back.

Let's fix it. Your 4-step plan:

  • Slow down. Hang up. Then call your loved one back on the number you already have for them.
  • Check with someone else. Call another family member or friend to see if the story is true. Yes, even if the caller begged you not to.
  • Pick a family code word. The FBI suggests a secret word or phrase that only your family knows. No code word? No money.
  • Never pay with gift cards, crypto, wires, or a payment app to someone who insists. Already paid? Act fast to try to get it back, and report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

The best part? The whole family is about to be around one table for Thanksgiving. That's the perfect time to pick your code word. It takes one minute.

And remember: the same trick works at the office. If a call feels urgent and secret, that's your cue to slow down and check.
