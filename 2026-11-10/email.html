Is that really your boss?

Your phone buzzes. "Hey, are you busy? I need a quick favor. I'm stuck in a meeting."

It's your boss. Or the owner. Or your department head. They need gift cards for the team. Right now. And can you keep it a surprise?

The truth is, it's not your boss at all. It's a scammer. The FTC warns about this exact trick, and with holiday parties and gifts around the corner, it sounds more believable than ever.

Here's how the scam works:

1
They pretend to be someone you trust.
A text, email, or call that looks like it's from your boss. Scammers even watch for new-hire posts online and go after new employees who want to make a good first impression.
2
They ask for an "urgent favor."
Buy gift cards. Maybe for a company event. Maybe they call them "Google certificates." They're too busy to talk. And it has to happen fast.
3
They ask for the numbers on the back.
Once you send the card numbers and PIN, the money is gone. That's the whole goal.

Let's fix it. Your 4-step check:

  • Pause. Rush + secrecy + gift cards = red flag. Every time.
  • Check with your boss directly. Call them on a number you already know is real, or walk down the hall. Don't reply to the text or email.
  • Never send gift card numbers or PINs. The FTC says it plainly: only scammers ask for gift card numbers and the PIN. No photos of the card, either.
  • Already sent them? Contact the gift card company right away, then report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. The sooner you report, the better your chances.

The best part? One quick phone call stops this scam cold. Your real boss will be glad you checked.

Managers, here's an easy win: tell your team you will never ask them to buy gift cards. Then everyone knows a request like that is fake.
